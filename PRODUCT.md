# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Professional women aged 35-55 living in Calgary, Airdrie, Cochrane, and Okotoks. Lawyers, doctors, founders, executives, senior professionals. High income, limited time. They juggle a boardroom, a parent pickup, and a dinner in the same week.

They are not looking for a makeover. They have a wardrobe; it isn't working. They want someone older-sister-competent to walk in, name what's wrong, and leave them with a closet that obeys for the next twelve months.

Their job-to-be-done: stop wasting twenty minutes every morning. Walk into a meeting, a court room, or a fundraiser knowing nothing needs to be thought about. Stop accumulating; start editing.

When they land on the site, they are likely between calls or on their phone after the kids are asleep. They are evaluating whether Anda is a serious practitioner or a personal-brand influencer. The site has thirty seconds to communicate the difference.

## Product Purpose

Anda Styling is a personal-styling practice based in Calgary, serving the metro and surrounding towns. Founder Anda was born in Rwanda, trained in Kigali, and has practised in Canada since 2018.

The site exists to convert qualified inquiries. Success looks like a calm, considered visitor moving from landing to booking the inquiry form without ever feeling sold to. A bad outcome is a visitor who reads the site as "another wedding-and-events stylist" or "another influencer with a closet". The site is the first proof that Anda is neither.

Secondary purpose: long-term brand asset. A site that ages well over five years, not one that needs a refresh every season.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **tactile · quiet · authored**.

- **Tactile**: the work is hands-on, in real closets, with real fabric. The site should feel made by a person, not assembled from a template. Texture, hand, slight imperfection, single-author voice.
- **Quiet**: restraint is the practice. Anda does not raise her voice. The site does not raise its voice. White space is generous; the page does not fight for attention.
- **Authored**: every section feels written by Anda, in Anda's voice, with a point of view. No "transforming your wardrobe journey" agency copy. First-person, calm, decisive.

Emotional goal: a visitor closes the tab and thinks "she sounds like the person I've been looking for", not "what a beautiful website".

## Anti-references

Strongest no: **magazine-cover editorial template**. The Klim-influenced display-serif + italic + mono-kicker + `§ 01` numbering lane is the AI default for fashion-adjacent brands in 2026. Do not land here even by accident. Mono labels are banned as decorative scaffolding; reserve mono only if a single deliberate use survives the audit.

Also no:
- Wedding-photographer beige + script. Cream, sage, soft-focus, "curated lifestyle" Pinterest aesthetic. Generic feminine-business default.
- Influencer / lifestyle blog. Big author headshot above the fold, peach gradients, "shop my closet" energy.
- Corporate-consulting deck. Navy + gold, stock photo of woman pointing at whiteboard, "transformative styling journey" copy.
- Drop-cap, italic-serif decorative reach. "Like you" treatment as ornament, not voice.
- Magazine masthead cosplay. `VOL.04 / ISSUE 12` framing for a service business.

Reference brands in roughly the right lane (study but do not copy):
- **Aesop** — restraint as voice, editorial weight without magazine cosplay, hand-written product cards.
- **The Row** — quiet authority. Photography does almost everything. Type does almost nothing.
- **Cos** — calm sans, image-led, generous spacing, no costume.
- **Toogood** — single-author atelier voice, tactile material world, photography that respects the maker.

Anti-references in adjacent lanes (avoid):
- Anthropologie / Free People marketing — too lifestyle, too whimsical.
- Glossier circa 2018 — pink millennial direct-to-consumer.
- Stitch Fix / Trunk Club — algorithmic service, not authored practice.
- Calgary local stylist competitors who lean wedding-and-events.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust before performance.** The job of every section is to answer "should a senior professional trust this person with their wardrobe". Not "look how cool this site is". If a design move serves the site's vanity instead of the visitor's evaluation, cut it.
2. **Restraint earned, not imitated.** Quiet luxury without copying The Row's template page-for-page. Every restrained move comes from Anda's actual practice (one careful client at a time, no upselling, no trends), not from a Pinterest board of restrained sites.
3. **The hand is visible.** Tactile, authored. Slight imperfection over corporate cleanliness. Photography of real garments and real spaces beats stock or 3D rendering. Type should feel set, not generated.
4. **Identity is structural, not decorative.** Anda's Rwandan-Canadian background, her studio in Calgary, her practice since 2018 — these inform voice and the breadth of the client portrait. They do not appear as ornament (no kente accents, no map-of-Rwanda flourishes, no "global perspective" copy).
5. **Service is the product.** Every section earns its place by moving a qualified visitor toward booking. No press ticker until press exists. No testimonials until clients have given quotes. No metrics until they are real. Empty space beats fabricated proof.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**WCAG 2.2 AA** as the launch floor:
- Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for body; ≥ 3:1 for large text.
- All interactive controls keyboard-reachable with visible `:focus-visible` styling.
- Proper landmarks (`<header>`, `<nav>`, `<main>`, `<footer>`) and a single `<h1>` per page.
- Form fields with associated `<label>`, error messages tied via `aria-describedby`.
- Alt text on every image, written in Anda's voice ("Cream linen overshirt and tailored wool trouser, March fitting"), not generic ("woman wearing clothes").
- Touch targets ≥ 44×44 px on mobile.

Audience-specific considerations:
- Demographic skews 35-55, so respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Fade-ins disabled when the OS asks. No autoplaying video.
- Site likely read in low-light evening conditions (on phone after kids in bed). Test contrast on dimmed screens, not just bright daylight.
- No carousels for primary content. Carousels fail keyboard users and time-pressed readers.

Color blindness: do not encode meaning by colour alone (e.g. accent for "available" + neutral for "booked"). Pair with text or icon.
